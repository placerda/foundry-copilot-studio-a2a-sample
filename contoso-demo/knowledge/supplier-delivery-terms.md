This fictional example is not a real purchase order or contract. The supplier's
availability is a stated proposal, not verified inventory or production data.

## Fictional proposal

The buyer requested 500 units with delivery due Friday. The supplier proposes
300 units on Friday and the remaining 200 on Monday. The proposal changes the
delivery schedule, even though the total quantity remains 500 units.

## Buyer acceptance conditions

The fictional buyer's default condition is complete delivery by the agreed due
date. A partial shipment does not by itself satisfy the original schedule.
It may be considered only when the buyer's authorized purchasing representative
approves the revised schedule in writing before dispatch. The approval must
identify the order, both quantities, both delivery dates, and any agreed change
to transport charges. Silence is not approval.

## Recommended next step

Submit the split-delivery proposal to the authorized purchasing representative.
Explain the Friday shortfall of 200 units and the proposed Monday balance.
Request written acceptance of the revised schedule and confirmation of any
transport-charge change. Until acceptance is recorded, describe the proposal as
pending, not accepted, and do not promise that the original terms are satisfied.

## Evidence limits

This source does not establish an actual order number, calendar dates, available
inventory, receiving capacity, penalty amounts, compensation, or a named
approver. It does not prove that an approval has been granted. The agents can
explain these fictional conditions but cannot approve a purchase-order change,
waive penalties, sign a contract, or dispatch goods.
